naskapi community boardwalk

A curved corten steel mural and six life-size caribou sculptures line the community boardwalk in Kawawachikamach.

original scope
Artistic conception of the corten steel artworks for the community boardwalk, a landscape architecture project by CCxA: a 35-foot-long mural and six caribou sculptures in three distinct forms.
expanded scope
Drawing on his experience delivering turnkey projects through K-One, the artist also took responsibility for technical design, handling, transportation and installation solutions, fabrication of all components, and delivery.

Central to Naskapi life and territory, the caribou becomes a presence along the boardwalk.

The mural traces a landscape, echoing the meaning of Naskapi: “people beyond the horizon.” Beginning with the image of a caribou overlooking the land, the composition grew through stories shared by community members. It brings together a wolf, a black bear, a fish, geese, Elders in a canoe, Fort Chimo and ancestral motifs. A ptarmigan is hidden within it, much as it would be in nature.

4.1 tonnes of steel, partly hand-finished by James Kennedy, then transported to Kawawachikamach by truck and train.

a co-creation process

The artworks were developed through a co-creation process with the Naskapi community. In February 2024, the artist travelled to Kawawachikamach to meet community members, council representatives and teachers. Each visual proposal and its subsequent revisions were presented to the band council for approval.

technical details
Each caribou is bolted to a custom steel base designed for handling, transportation and installation. The sculptures are freestanding, without ground anchors; stability is provided by steel ballast built into the base.
